| D10 | Requirement-level target date, and the Planning screen | **add `requirement.target_date`; a requirement with no explicit date falls back to a date derived from its stage threshold** | 2026-08-17 | Requested by the product owner, who wants a planning surface answering "when is this requirement due" with a horizon of a month, a week, or a number of days. Raised the conflict first: §13 of the build specification lists **effort estimation**, **Gantt charts / resource levelling / capacity planning** and **sprint management** as explicitly out of scope, "recorded so they are not rebuilt by accident", and the spec mentions no date on `requirement` anywhere. The distinction this decision rests on: a **due date is a commitment about when**, not an estimate of **how long** — Vyoog already stores `release.target_date` (VYB-0372) and already merges dated things into My Work → Calendar, so a date is not itself the excluded thing. **What this does not authorise:** duration bars, capacity or resource levelling, any per-person load ranking, and any figure derived by converting scope signals into days — Principle 7 and §13 both stand unchanged, and the Planning screen renders point markers rather than bars for exactly that reason. **Fallback, not invention:** where no explicit date is set, the screen shows `entered-stage date + app_config.stage_stall_threshold_days[status]`, which is the same configured expectation `TaskService.stalled()` has always used for the Pipeline's stalled markers — labelled as derived so it is never confused with a date a person committed to. A requirement committed to a release shows that release's target date, rendered as borrowed with its source named (Principle 8). **Consequence:** `requirement` now carries a nullable `target_date` that no other module reads; if the delivery tool later becomes the system of record for dates, this column is the thing to retire, not the derived fallback. |
| D11 | Who may assign a requirement to whom | **a team member carries a role (`LEAD` or `MEMBER`); only a lead of the assignee's team may assign to them — but a team with no lead stays open to everyone** | 2026-08-19 | Requested by the product owner ("now come with role, assigned by assigned to i want to set"). `team`/`team_member` already existed (VYB-0464) but carried no role, so there was no way to express "who may hand this person work" and the two prior sessions' assignment specs (§11–§12) could not be implemented at all. **The rule is deliberately asymmetric:** where a team has a lead, only that lead may assign; where a team has none — or the assignee is in no team — anyone may. A rule that refused whenever no lead was configured would make assignment impossible on a fresh install and on every team nobody has got round to configuring, which is a lockout rather than a permission model. The unled case is *shown* as unled in Administration → Teams (Principle 8) rather than left looking configured. A platform `ADMINISTRATOR` bypasses the check, resolved through the existing `GrantResolver` rather than a second notion of privilege. **`planning_assignment.assigned_by_role` stores the assigner's role at the moment of assignment** instead of resolving it later: a lead who steps down would otherwise retroactively turn every assignment they ever made into one made by a member, which makes the history lie about what was authorised at the time. **Demotion is refused when it would leave a team with members and no lead** — otherwise a routine role change silently reopens that team to everyone. **What this does not authorise:** no reporting line, no hierarchy of leads, no capacity or allocation, and no per-person workload ranking — §13 and Principle 7 are untouched; a lead answers one question only. **Not addressed:** notifying anybody of an assignment (held at the product owner's request — no email infrastructure exists), and whether roles should eventually come from Keycloak groups rather than being set in Vyoog. |
| D13 | Per-phase due dates, and client confirmation of the overall date | **three phase deadlines (`requirement_phase`) gated behind a recorded client confirmation of `requirement.target_date`, where "confirmed" is a predicate against the date currently set** | 2026-08-19 | Requested by the product owner: an overall date is agreed with the client, and only then split into development, testing and implementation deadlines. **Raised the §13 conflict first**, as D10 did: §13 excludes effort estimation, Gantt charts and capacity planning, and three ordered dates on one requirement sit closer to that line than one date does, because two dates on a timeline imply a duration. The distinction held: these are **deadlines recorded as points**, and nothing computes or renders the interval between two of them. The UI is a labelled grid, explicitly not a track, for that reason. **Confirmation is a predicate, not a flag** — deliberately the same shape as `requirement_verification_state` (Principle 3): a date is Confirmed only when a confirmation row exists for the date *currently* set, so moving the date invalidates the agreement by itself, with no flag to clear and no job to run. A client who agreed to 10 October has not agreed to 24 November. **Vyoog has no client entity and no client login** — the nearest thing is an `EXTERNAL` user with a time-limited grant (VYB-0702) — so `requirement_date_commitment` records that *one of our people obtained* a confirmation: `recorded_by` is always the Vyoog user, `confirmed_by_name` is the client-side person, and the modal carries a permanent, non-dismissible line saying the client is not signing there. Presenting it otherwise would make it a forgery rather than a record. **Phase assignees are not stored in the phase table**: `requirement.developer_id`/`tester_id` already exist and `TaskService` derives four of its seven task kinds from them, so a second copy would drift from the one that actually puts work in front of a person; only `implementer_id` is new. **Who actually approved, verified or deployed stays read-only**, derived by `LifecycleHistoryService` from review signatures, verifications and deployments — the product owner asked for it to be editable and it is deliberately not, because letting somebody rewrite who signed off after the fact destroys the traceability chain the product exists to keep. Assigned people are editable; actual actors are a record. **Out-of-order phases are refused** (testing before development is a contradiction, not a plan with a problem); **a phase later than the overall date only warns**, because refusing it would push people to quietly move the committed date so the form submits. **Not addressed:** release-level confirmation with per-requirement inheritance (recommended and deferred), and notifying anyone that a confirmation has gone stale — notifications remain held at the product owner's request. |
